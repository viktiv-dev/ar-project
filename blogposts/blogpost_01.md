# Blog Post #01 - Introduction to Extended Reality
### Author: Viktor Ivaylov Ivanov
## What and Why of XR

Extended Reality (XR) is an umbrella term that includes Augmented Reality, Virtual Reality, and Mixed Reality . These technologies exist on a spectrum between the physical and digital worlds. AR overlays digital content onto the real world, VR fully immerses the user in a virtual environment and MR blends physical and digital elements in a more persistent and interactive way. XR is used as a collective term to describe these technologies and their shared goal of enhancing human perception through immersive digital experiences.

Compared to traditional desktop or mobile applications, XR applications operate in three-dimensional space and rely on more natural forms of input such as head movement, hand tracking, spatial interaction, etc. This allows XR to represent information in ways that are often more intuitive, especially when dealing with complex or spatial concepts. However, XR also comes with disadvantages, including higher hardware requirements, comfort challenges, and increased development complexity.

XR has a wide range of use cases, including education, training and simulation, healthcare, architecture, and entertainment. It is particularly effective in scenarios where understanding scale, depth, or physical relationships is important. By allowing users to interact directly with content, XR can improve engagement and learning outcomes compared to passive, screen-based experiences.

A key concept in XR is immersion and presence, which is the feeling of being inside an environment rather than observing it from the outside. This sense of presence is what gives XR its unique value but it also places higher demands on interaction design and performance. Poorly designed interactions can quickly break immersion or cause discomfort.

While current XR technology still has limitations in terms of comfort, accessibility, and hardware constraints, it already demonstrates strong potential. In an ideal future, XR devices would be lightweight, affordable, and seamlessly integrated into everyday life. Even in its current form, XR is capable of solving problems that traditional applications struggle with by turning abstract information into interactive, spatial experiences.
