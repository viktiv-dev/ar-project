# Blog Post #7 - Designing the Virtual Lab Space & Showcase
### Author: Viktor Ivaylov Ivanov

## Designing the VR Laboratory

With the core interaction systems in place, the final stage of development focused on designing the virtual laboratory environment. The goal was not realism, but clarity and atmosphere. The lab was designed as a clean, slightly sci-fi inspired space, reinforcing the idea that the user is experimenting in a controlled, futuristic environment rather than a real-world classroom.

A minimal architectural layout was chosen, using simple walls, a roof, and defined interaction zones. This helped prevent visual clutter and ensured that the user’s attention remained on the atoms, molecules, and interactive stations.

## Points of Interest & Learning Anchors

To make the space feel purposeful and educational, several points of interest were added around the lab. These included a large periodic table display, as well as pedestals showcasing completed molecules.Presenting molecules in this way reinforces spatial understanding and gives users time to inspect shape that is difficult to achieve using traditional 2D representations.

## Interaction-Driven Environment Design

All interactive elements in the lab were designed to feel physical and intentional. Instead of relying on floating menus, interactions are embedded directly into the environment. Buttons exist as tangible objects that can be pressed, and holographic displays are projected into space rather than overlaid onto the user’s view.

This design choice helps maintain immersion and reduces cognitive load. Users interact with the lab in the same way they would interact with real equipment: by reaching, pressing, grabbing, and observing—rather than navigating abstract UI elements.

## Visual Feedback & Holographic Displays

To enhance the sci-fi aesthetic, holographic UI elements were introduced. These displays use animated visuals, subtle jitter, and scrolling textures to create the illusion of projected data rather than flat screens. Information panels feel like tools within the lab rather than external interfaces.

This approach also helps distinguish informational content from physical objects, making it immediately clear what can be interacted with and what is meant for observation.

## Managing Physical Clutter

As users are encouraged to freely grab and drop atoms, it became important to manage physical clutter within the lab. Atoms that fall onto the floor and remain unused are automatically removed after a short delay.
